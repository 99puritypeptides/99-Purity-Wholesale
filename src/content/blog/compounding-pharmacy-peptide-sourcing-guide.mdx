# What Compounding Pharmacies Should Know About Sourcing Research-Grade Peptides

Compounding pharmacies occupy a unique position in the U.S. peptide supply chain. As licensed facilities subject to state board oversight and — for some — 503B outsourcing facility regulations, compounders face specific procurement requirements that standard B2B buyers do not. This guide is written specifically for pharmacy procurement officers, compounding directors, and quality assurance teams evaluating wholesale peptide supply relationships.

## The Compounding Pharmacy Market for Peptides

Peptide research compounds represent a growing category of interest for U.S. compounding pharmacies. The research landscape around compounds such as Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, BPC-157, TB-500, and growth hormone secretagogues like Ipamorelin and CJC-1295 has expanded rapidly, driving procurement demand from facilities across the country.

For compounders, sourcing peptides for research supply requires:
- Reliable domestic sourcing
- Verified purity documentation meeting pharmacy procurement standards
- Batch-matched COA documentation for records
- Responsive supplier relationships that can support volume fluctuations

## Quality Standards Compounders Must Demand

### ≥99% Purity via Independent Testing

Compounding facilities require a higher standard of documentation than general research buyers. A batch-matched Certificate of Analysis showing ≥99% purity via High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) and Mass Spectrometry (MS), issued by an independent accredited U.S. laboratory, is the minimum acceptable documentation standard.

Reject any supplier offering:
- Generic or undated COAs
- In-house testing only (no third-party verification)
- Purity stated as a range below 99%
- COAs not matched to the specific batch being supplied

### Manufacturing Traceability

Compounders should require disclosure of manufacturing origin. U.S.-synthesized peptides from domestic facilities offer superior traceability and eliminate international supply chain variables including:
- Customs seizure and border delays
- Unknown synthesis conditions and reagent quality
- No recourse in quality disputes
- Cold-chain integrity failures during international transit

### Documentation for Procurement Records

Compounding pharmacy procurement records should include:
- Supplier name, address, and contact information
- Product name, CAS number, and batch number
- COA with testing laboratory name and accreditation
- Date of manufacture and expiration
- Quantity and specifications of each order

## Sourcing Categories Relevant to Compounding Research Programs

### GLP-1 Metabolic Peptides
Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide, and Cagrilintide represent the highest-demand category for pharmacy procurement research programs. These long-acting GLP-1 and dual/triple receptor agonist compounds require verified purity documentation given the precision required in research applications.

### Healing and Recovery Compounds
BPC-157 (CAS: 137525-51-0), TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4, CAS: 77591-33-4), and combination protocols are commonly sourced by compounding facilities supporting musculoskeletal research programs.

### Growth Hormone Secretagogues
Ipamorelin, CJC-1295 (with and without DAC), Sermorelin Acetate, and Tesamorelin are research compounds in the growth hormone peptide category with established wholesale procurement demand from pharmacy operations.

### Anti-Aging and Longevity Research
Epithalon, MOTS-c, Thymalin, Thymosin Alpha-1, NAD+, and AOD9604 represent the longevity research category, increasingly relevant to pharmacy research supply programs.

## Evaluating Wholesale Peptide Suppliers: A Due Diligence Framework

Before establishing a wholesale peptide supply relationship, compounding procurement teams should verify:

**Supplier Qualifications:**
- Is the supplier a U.S.-based manufacturer or a domestic importer/repackager?
- Can the supplier provide facility information?
- Does the supplier have a verifiable track record with licensed facilities?

**Documentation Standards:**
- Does the supplier provide batch-specific COAs for every lot?
- Are COAs issued by independent, named accredited laboratories?
- Can COAs be verified or audited by the buyer?

**Operational Reliability:**
- What are the MOQ requirements and volume tier structure?
- What is typical order processing and fulfillment turnaround?
- Is there a dedicated account manager or B2B support contact?

**Compliance Posture:**
- Does the supplier clearly position products as research use only?
- Does the supplier require buyer attestation of licensed status?
- Are all sales B2B only with no consumer sales?

## Payment and Order Management for Pharmacy Procurement

Wholesale peptide suppliers serving compounding pharmacies typically accept:
- Bank transfer (ACH/Wire) — preferred for institutional procurement
- Zelle — for smaller or faster transactions
- Bitcoin — available from some specialty suppliers

Compounders should request net terms discussions for established high-volume accounts, as many domestic wholesale suppliers offer payment flexibility for verified pharmacy clients.
