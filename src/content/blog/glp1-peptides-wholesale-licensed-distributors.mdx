The GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonist class has become one of the most researched peptide categories in modern biochemistry. ## What Are GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Peptides?

GLP-1 receptor agonists are a class of peptide compounds that interact with the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor, a G protein-coupled receptor expressed throughout the body. Research into this class has expanded significantly over the past decade, making it one of the most actively studied peptide families in academic and institutional research settings.

The primary compounds in this research category include:

- **Semaglutide** — A long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist studied for its receptor binding profile and metabolic signaling effects
- **Tirzepatide** — A dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist; one of the most actively researched dual-agonist compounds
- **Retatrutide** — A triple hormone receptor agonist (GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon) representing the next generation of metabolic research compounds
- **Cagrilintide** — An amylin analogue often studied in combination protocols
- **Dulaglutide** — A long-acting GLP-1 receptor agonist with a distinct pharmacokinetic profile
