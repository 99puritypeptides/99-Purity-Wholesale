# The Ultimate Guide to Peptide Reconstitution and Storage

Proper handling, reconstitution (mixing), and storage are critical steps in maintaining the structural integrity and biological activity of research peptides. A high-purity compound can be rendered useless by improper solvents, excessive agitation, or incorrect storage temperatures.

This guide provides researchers with best practices for handling lyophilized peptides in a laboratory setting.

## What is Lyophilization?

Most research peptides are shipped as a lyophilized powder. **Lyophilization** (freeze-drying) removes water from the compound while preserving its chemical structure. This process extends the shelf life and prevents degradation during transit at ambient temperatures.

However, to use the peptide in experiments, it must be returned to a liquid state—a process called **reconstitution**.

## The Reconstitution Protocol

To reconstitute a peptide, you need a sterile solvent. 

### 1. Choosing the Solvent
*   **Bacteriostatic Water (BAC Water):** The gold standard for multiple-use vials. It contains 0.9% benzyl alcohol, which inhibits bacterial growth, allowing the reconstituted peptide to be stored safely in the refrigerator for weeks.
*   **Sterile Water / Saline:** Used for single-use applications where the entire vial will be utilized immediately. It does not contain preservatives and will grow bacteria if stored after opening.

### 2. The Mixing Process
Peptide bonds are fragile. The physical force of a solvent hitting the powder directly, or vigorous shaking, can cause the peptide chains to shear (break apart), destroying the compound.

*   **Equalize Pressure:** Ensure the vial is at room temperature before mixing.
*   **Inject Slowly:** When adding BAC water, angle the needle so the liquid drips slowly down the glass side of the vial, rather than blasting directly onto the powder plug.
*   **Do Not Shake:** Never shake the vial. Gently swirl or roll the vial between your fingers until the powder is fully dissolved and the liquid is clear.

## Calculating Doses (The Math)

When you reconstitute a peptide, the concentration depends on the amount of solvent added. 
For example, if you have a **5mg** vial of a peptide and you add **2mL** of BAC water:

*   Total Peptide: 5mg (5,000 mcg)
*   Total Volume: 2mL (200 units on a standard syringe)
*   Concentration: 5,000 mcg / 200 units = **25 mcg per unit**

Always document the exact volume of solvent used on the vial label for accurate downstream dosing.

## Storage Guidelines

Temperature control is the most critical factor in peptide stability.

### Storing Unmixed (Lyophilized) Peptides
*   **Short Term (Weeks to Months):** Store at 4°C (refrigerated) away from direct light.
*   **Long Term (Up to 2 Years):** Store at -20°C (freezer). 
*   *Note on Freezing:* Minimize freeze-thaw cycles. Do not take a vial in and out of the freezer repeatedly, as condensation can introduce moisture and degrade the powder.

### Storing Mixed (Reconstituted) Peptides
*   **Always Refrigerate:** Once mixed, peptides must be stored at 4°C. 
*   **Do Not Freeze:** Never freeze a reconstituted peptide. Freezing water crystals will destroy the delicate peptide chains.
*   **Shelf Life:** Most peptides remain stable for **2 to 4 weeks** after reconstitution with BAC water.
